Signs of a tight pelvic floor in men, and what to do

A tight pelvic floor is one that struggles to relax. Signs can include pelvic or groin pain, discomfort when sitting, a frequent or urgent need to pee, and soreness after squeezing. If this sounds like you, more kegels may add tension. Focus on relaxation and reverse kegels, and see a GP or pelvic floor physiotherapist.

Weak or tight: why it matters

Most kegel advice assumes the muscle is weak. But a pelvic floor can also be overactive, holding tension all the time. Squeezing harder trains the wrong thing for a tight muscle.

Common signs to look out for

  • Aching or pain in the pelvis, groin, testicles or lower back.
  • Discomfort when sitting for long periods.
  • Needing to pee often or urgently.
  • Feeling sore or tense after kegels.
  • Finding it hard to fully let go down there.

These signs have several possible causes, so they are a reason to get checked, not a diagnosis.

What helps a tight pelvic floor?

Relaxation comes first: slow belly breathing, reverse kegels and gentle positions. A pelvic floor physiotherapist can assess you properly and guide treatment.

How Kegzo handles this

Kegzo asks four quick safety questions before you start. If your answers suggest tension, it puts you on the Relax track, which is built mostly from reverse kegels and gentle holds, and suggests seeing a professional.

Frequently asked questions

Can I switch tracks later?

Yes. You can change between the Strength and Relax tracks at any time in the app.

Should I stop kegels completely?

If squeezing causes pain or soreness, stop and get advice. Gentle relaxation work is usually a safer place to start.
