Kegel exercises for men: the complete beginner guide

Kegel exercises for men are squeezes of the pelvic floor, the sling of muscle between the tailbone and the pubic bone. You draw the muscle up and in, hold, then let it go completely. Most programmes train a few times a week or daily for around three months, starting lying down and progressing to sitting, standing and walking.

What is the pelvic floor?

The pelvic floor is a group of muscles that runs like a hammock across the bottom of your pelvis. In men it supports the bladder and bowel and includes the muscles at the base of the penis.

Like any muscle, it can be trained for strength, speed, stamina and, just as important, the ability to relax.

How do you do a kegel correctly?

  1. Get comfortable lying on your back with your knees bent.
  2. Relax your belly, bum and thighs.
  3. Draw up and in, as if stopping wind and the flow of urine at the same time. Only imagine it.
  4. Notice the base of the penis pull in slightly and the testicles lift. That is the right muscle.
  5. Hold for a few seconds while breathing normally, then let go completely.

If your bum clenches, your belly pushes out or you hold your breath, ease off and try again with less effort.

How often should men do kegels?

Little and often works best. A short session of five to ten minutes on most days is enough for most people. Most structured programmes run for around three months. Consistency matters far more than squeezing hard.

What does a good programme include?

A balanced programme trains several qualities, not just one long squeeze:

  • Strength: firm holds of 5 to 10 seconds.
  • Speed: quick flicks with a full release.
  • Stamina: lighter holds kept for longer.
  • Control: lifting and lowering in stages.
  • Release: reverse kegels, where the muscle softens and drops.

Positions get harder over time: lying down first, then seated, standing and finally walking.

Who should be careful with kegels?

If you have pelvic pain, discomfort when sitting, or a feeling that you cannot relax down there, your pelvic floor may already be too tight. Squeezing harder can make that worse. Start with reverse kegels and speak to a GP or pelvic floor physiotherapist.

Frequently asked questions

Can I do kegels anywhere?

Yes, once you know the feeling. Nobody can see a kegel, so seated and standing practice can happen at a desk or in a queue.

Should I feel kegels in my stomach?

No. Your belly should stay relaxed. Feeling it in your stomach usually means the abs are helping too much.

Are kegels only for older men?

No. Men of any age can train the pelvic floor, though men with signs of a tight pelvic floor should focus on relaxation first.
