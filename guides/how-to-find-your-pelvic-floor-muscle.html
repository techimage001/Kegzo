How to find your pelvic floor muscle as a man

To find your pelvic floor, relax your belly, bum and thighs, then gently draw up and in as if stopping wind and the flow of urine at the same time. The right muscle makes the base of the penis pull in slightly and the testicles lift. If your bum clenches or you hold your breath, you are using the wrong muscles.

Which cues work best for men?

  • Stop the wind: imagine holding in wind in a quiet room.
  • Stop the flow: imagine stopping urine midstream. Only imagine it, do not practise while you pee.
  • Shorten and lift: imagine the penis shortening and the testicles lifting toward your body.

Pelvic floor physiotherapists use these same cues when teaching men.

What does using the wrong muscle feel like?

The most common mix-ups are:

  • Clenching the glutes, so your bum lifts or tightens.
  • Pushing the belly out or pulling it in hard.
  • Squeezing the inner thighs together.
  • Holding your breath.

Use about half effort while you learn. A small, precise squeeze in the right place beats a big squeeze everywhere.

How can I check I have it right?

Try a three-second squeeze lying down, then let go. Check three things: your bum stayed soft, you kept breathing, and your belly did not push out. Looking in a mirror can help, as you should see the base of the penis draw in and the testicles rise slightly.

What if I cannot feel anything?

That is common at the start. Try lying down with your knees bent, breathe out slowly as you squeeze, and keep the effort gentle. If after a couple of weeks you still cannot feel it, a pelvic floor physiotherapist can teach it hands-on.

Frequently asked questions

Is it bad to stop urine to practise?

Using it once to understand the feeling is fine for many people, but regularly stopping the flow while you pee is not recommended.

Should the squeeze be hard?

No. Start gentle, around half effort. Strength comes later.
