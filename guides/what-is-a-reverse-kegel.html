What is a reverse kegel, and how do you do one?

A reverse kegel is the opposite of a squeeze. As you breathe in, you let the pelvic floor soften, widen and drop slightly, like the moment you start to pee, without pushing or straining. It trains the muscle to fully relax, which matters because a muscle that cannot let go does not work well.

How do you do a reverse kegel?

  1. Lie on your back or sit comfortably and relax your shoulders.
  2. Breathe in slowly and deeply into your belly.
  3. As you breathe in, let the area between your legs soften and gently drop. Do not push.
  4. Breathe out and let it return to neutral without squeezing.

Why does relaxation matter?

Many men carry tension in the pelvic floor without knowing it. A tight pelvic floor can cause discomfort and may make it harder for the muscle to work properly. Training the release keeps strength training balanced.

Who should focus on reverse kegels?

If you have pelvic pain, discomfort when sitting, a frequent urge to pee, or a feeling that you cannot relax down there, focus on reverse kegels first and speak to a GP or pelvic floor physiotherapist. Kegzo puts you on its Relax track if your safety check suggests this.

How is it different from pushing?

A reverse kegel is gentle and happens with the in-breath. Bearing down or straining is not the goal, and you should never feel pressure or pain.
