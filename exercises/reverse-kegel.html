Reverse kegel: how to do it

A reverse kegel is the opposite of a squeeze. As you breathe in, the pelvic floor softens, widens and drops slightly. Training the release matters because a muscle that cannot fully relax does not work well, and many men hold too much tension down there.

Try Reverse kegel with the coachCurrently free. Start straight away on any phone.

How do you do reverse kegel?

  1. Breathe in slowly into your belly.
  2. As you breathe in, let the pelvic floor soften, widen and drop, the opposite of a squeeze.
  3. Breathe out and let it return to neutral without squeezing.

What should you feel?

A gentle opening and heaviness low down, like the moment you start to pee.

What should you avoid?

  • Pushing or straining hard
  • Squeezing on the way back
  • Holding your breath
Coach tip: A muscle that can fully relax works better. Never skip this one.

When does it appear in the programme?

It unlocks in week 1 of the programme and is first practised lying down. Sets: 5 slow drops.

Frequently asked questions

Is a reverse kegel the same as pushing?

No. It is a gentle letting go on the in-breath, not straining or bearing down.

How often should I do reverse kegels?

In Kegzo they come in every session, and they make up most of the Relax track.
