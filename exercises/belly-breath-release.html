Belly breath release: how to do it

This bonus exercise trains the release side of pelvic floor work. Breathing slowly into your belly lets the pelvic floor soften and drop as you breathe in, which is the movement behind a reverse kegel. A muscle that can fully let go works better when it needs to squeeze.

Try Belly breath release with the coachCurrently free. Start straight away on any phone.

How do you do belly breath release?

  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, hands resting on your belly.
  2. Breathe in slowly through your nose and let your belly rise under your hands.
  3. As your belly rises, let the pelvic floor soften and drop.
  4. Breathe out slowly and let everything settle, without squeezing.

What should you feel?

Your belly rising under your hands and a soft, heavy feeling low down as you breathe in.

What should you avoid?

  • Pushing or straining down
  • Lifting your chest instead of your belly
  • Rushing the breath
Coach tip: Slow is everything. Four seconds in, five seconds out.

When does it appear in the programme?

It is a bonus exercise you can try any time, practised lying down. Sets: 6 slow belly breaths.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the same as a reverse kegel?

It is the breathing that sits underneath a reverse kegel. It is a good way to learn the drop if a reverse kegel feels hard to find.

Should I push down?

No. Nothing is pushed. The pelvic floor softens on its own as your belly fills.
